## 1. Need statement

### 1.1 The problem

**A. Many persons have dementia. The number increases every year.**

1. In 2021, approximately 57 million persons in the world had dementia. Each year, there are approximately 10 million new cases [[1]](#ref-1).
2. More than 60% of these persons live in low-income and middle-income countries [[1]](#ref-1).
3. The WHO expects 78 million persons with dementia in 2030 and 139 million in 2050 [[2]](#ref-2).

**B. Most persons with dementia do not get a diagnosis.**

1. Approximately 75% of persons with dementia in the world do not have a diagnosis [[3]](#ref-3).
2. In low-income countries, this number can be up to 90% [[3]](#ref-3).
3. Without a diagnosis, a person cannot get treatment, support or a care plan early.

**C. Persons can decrease their risk.**

1. The Lancet Commission (2024) found 14 risk factors that persons can change. Examples are hearing loss, physical inactivity, social isolation and high LDL cholesterol [[4]](#ref-4).
2. If these 14 risk factors are removed, approximately 45% of dementia cases can possibly be prevented or delayed [[4]](#ref-4). This number is a calculation. It assumes that each risk factor is a cause.
3. In the FINGER trial, a 2-year program improved the cognition of older persons at risk a little. The program had diet, exercise, brain training and vascular risk checks [[5]](#ref-5).
4. In the ACTIVE trial, older persons who did speed-of-processing training had a 29% lower risk of dementia after 10 years [[6]](#ref-6).

**D. The current tools have problems.**

1. Many brain-training products make large claims. In 2016, the US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) said that Lumosity made deceptive claims. One claim was that the games delay cognitive decline and dementia. Lumos Labs paid $2 million [[8]](#ref-8).
2. A large scientific review (2016) found good evidence that persons get better at the trained games. It found little evidence that this helps in daily life [[7]](#ref-7).
3. Many tools cost money each month or each year (see [3. Competitor analysis](#3-competitor-analysis)).
4. Many tools are not made for persons with early dementia. Small text, many buttons and timers make them difficult to use.
5. Most older adults have a smartphone. In the USA, approximately 3 out of 4 adults aged 65 and older own a smartphone [[9]](#ref-9). A web app can thus reach many older persons.

<br>

### 1.2 The need

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Persons who are worried about their memory, healthy persons who want to protect their brain, and persons with early dementia need **one tool that is free, private and easy to use**.
>
> The tool must help them check their memory, practise every day and see changes early. It must tell them clearly when to speak to a doctor.

<br>

### 1.3 What aivOavi does for this need

| # | Need | What aivOavi does |
|:-:|---|---|
| 1 | Early signs of change | A Brain Check-up every 4 weeks. A trend card that turns orange when scores stay lower for 3 weeks. |
| 2 | A path to the doctor | A printed summary for the doctor. The app tells the user to speak to a doctor. It does not give a diagnosis. |
| 3 | Daily practice | 3 short games every day, with a garden, badges and levels. |
| 4 | Easy to use for persons with dementia | Easy mode: one big button, no timers, no scores, large text, read aloud, **▼ START HERE** and **■ END** markers. |
| 5 | Privacy | No account and no server. All data is locked (encrypted) on the device. |
| 6 | No cost and no installation | It is a free web app. It works in all modern browsers. |

<br>

### 1.4 Limits of aivOavi

1. aivOavi is not a medical device. Nobody did a clinical validation of the check-up. The limits for "green" and "orange" are not clinically validated.
2. Nobody proved that the aivOavi games prevent dementia. The scientific review [[7]](#ref-7) shows that game training does not always help in daily life.
3. aivOavi does not replace a doctor, a memory clinic or the lifestyle changes from the Lancet Commission [[4]](#ref-4).

## 2. Who the app is for

aivOavi has 3 paths. You select a path on the first screen. Two more persons get help from the app: the caregiver and the doctor.

These persons are examples. They are not real. Their names are also in the screenshots.

| # | | Person | Role | Path or area |
|:-:|:-:|---|---|---|
| 1 | 🩺 | **Sam, 66**, retired bookkeeper | **The Checker:** wants to know if their memory changes | *Check my memory* |
| 2 | 💪 | **Rosa, 54**, nurse | **The Preventer:** is healthy and wants to stay healthy | *Keep my brain strong* |
| 3 | 🌱 | **Grandpa Joe, 78**, has early dementia | **The Trainee:** needs easy practice every day | *Daily brain training* (Easy mode) |
| 4 | 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 | **Priya, 45**, the daughter of Joe | **The Caregiver:** helps Joe and looks at the results | Caregiver area |
| 5 | 👩‍⚕️ | **Dr. Mensah**, family doctor | **The Reader:** reads the printed summary | Doctor summary (paper or PDF) |

<br>

### 2.1 Sam: the Checker

**Problem:** Sam forgets names. Sam is worried. Sam does not know if this is normal at this age.

**What Sam does:**

1. Sam selects **Check my memory**.
2. Sam does the **Brain Check-up**. It takes approximately 8 minutes.
3. Sam does the check-up again every 4 weeks.

**How Sam gets help:**

1. Sam gets a clear result:
   - **Green ✅** means "Your results are good".
   - **Orange ⚠️** means "Some areas need attention".
2. The first check-up is the **starting point**. The app compares the next check-ups with this starting point. It does not compare Sam with other persons.
3. If a result is orange, Sam prints a **summary for the doctor**. Sam gives this summary to the doctor.
4. Other persons cannot see the results. Sam decides who sees them.

<br>

### 2.2 Rosa: the Preventer

**Problem:** The mother of Rosa had dementia. Rosa is healthy. Rosa wants a short brain exercise every day.

**What Rosa does:**

1. Rosa selects **Keep my brain strong**.
2. Rosa sets a reminder for the evening.
3. Rosa plays **3 short games every day**. The app selects the games.
4. Sometimes Rosa looks at **My Brain Journey**.

**How Rosa gets help:**

1. The app is like a game:
   - Rosa gets points (XP) and levels, from Seed to Mighty Oak.
   - Rosa gets a counter of "days in a row". The counter accepts 1 rest day each week.
   - The **Brain Garden** of Rosa grows one plant each day.
   - Rosa gets badges and sees personal best scores.
2. The games change their difficulty (levels 1 to 6). They are never too easy and never too difficult.
3. Each day, the app adds a game for the **weakest brain area** of Rosa.
4. Rosa sees the trend:
   - A **green** card shows "Your scores go up!".
   - An **orange** card shows when the scores stay 10% (or more) lower than the starting point for 3 weeks.

<br>

### 2.3 Grandpa Joe: the Trainee

**Problem:** Joe has early dementia. Many apps are too difficult for Joe. They have too many buttons, small text and timers.

**What Joe does:**

1. Joe opens the app.
2. Joe touches the big button **▶ Play today's games**. The text **▼ START HERE** is above this button.
3. Joe plays 3 games.
4. Joe sees **■ You are finished for today**. Joe closes the app.

**How Joe gets help:**

1. **Easy mode** shows only one big button:
   - There are no timers and no scores.
   - The maximum level is 3. Joe gets approximately 80% of the answers correct.
2. The app never says "wrong". It says "Try the next one" and shows the correct answer.
3. The text is large: 22 to 30 pixels. The buttons are large: minimum 72 pixels. Each button has an icon and a word.
4. There are no swipes and no long presses.
5. The **🔊 Read** button reads each screen aloud. The device makes the voice. No data goes to the internet.
6. Joe practises memory, attention, the date and time, and names and faces.
7. The garden gets one new plant each day. Joe sees the progress without numbers.

<br>

### 2.4 Priya: the Caregiver

**Problem:** Priya lives 30 minutes away from Joe. Priya wants to know if Joe plays every day, and how the results of Joe change.

**What Priya does:**

1. Priya installs aivOavi on the tablet of Joe.
2. Priya selects **Daily brain training**.
3. Priya adds a **PIN**.
4. Priya adds the daily reminder to the calendar of Joe.
5. Sometimes Priya opens the **Caregiver area** to look at the results.

**How Priya gets help:**

1. Priya can turn **Easy mode** on or off. Priya can change the main goal.
2. Priya sees **detailed progress**: a score for each brain area, tables and all check-ups.
3. Priya can make a **locked backup**. With the backup, Priya moves the data of Joe to a new device.
4. Priya can **delete all data**.
5. The orange card gives Priya an early sign. Priya can then speak to the doctor of Joe.

<br>

### 2.5 Dr. Mensah: the Reader

**Problem:** A short visit gives the doctor only one result. One result does not show a change over time.

**What Dr. Mensah does:** Dr. Mensah does not use the app. The patient brings the **printed doctor summary**.

**The summary shows:**

1. The date and the score for each area of each check-up.
2. A chart of the average score for each week, with the starting point.
3. A clear note: "These results are not a diagnosis."

**How Dr. Mensah gets help:**

1. The summary shows results from many weeks at home, not one result from a short visit.
2. This helps the doctor decide if the patient needs a full memory test.

## 3. Competitor analysis

### 3.1 The products we compared

We compared aivOavi with 6 products:

1. Three products are for brain training: **Lumosity**, **BrainHQ** and **Elevate**.
2. One product is for training and testing: **CogniFit**.
3. Two products are for memory screening: **SAGE** and **BrainCheck**.

The data comes from the websites and app store pages of the products (see [9. References](#9-references)). We checked this data in November 2026. Prices and functions can change. Make sure that you read the current page of each product.

| # | Product | Type | Cost | Memory screening | Daily training | Made for |
|:-:|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **aivOavi** | Web app (all browsers) | Free | Yes: Brain Check-up every 4 weeks, trend card | Yes: 3 games each day, Easy mode | Checkers, healthy adults, persons with early dementia, caregivers |
| 1 | **Lumosity** [[13]](#ref-13) | Phone app and website | Free basic version; paid premium subscription | No | Yes: brain games | General public |
| 2 | **BrainHQ** (Posit Science) [[11]](#ref-11) | Phone app and website | $14 each month or $96 each year (USA) [[11]](#ref-11) | No | Yes: brain exercises, including the speed training from the ACTIVE trial [[6]](#ref-6) | General public, adults |
| 3 | **Elevate** [[14]](#ref-14) | Phone app | Free basic version; paid subscription with a 7-day trial | No | Yes: brain games | General public |
| 4 | **CogniFit** [[12]](#ref-12) | Phone app and website | Free to download; paid subscriptions; the full assessment is a separate purchase | Yes: paid cognitive assessment | Yes: brain games | General public |
| 5 | **SAGE test** (Ohio State University) [[10]](#ref-10) | Paper test (4 forms); a tablet version is sold by BrainTest | Paper version: free for personal clinical use | Yes: one test for mild cognitive impairment and early dementia | No | Persons who want a self-test, doctors |
| 6 | **BrainCheck** [[15]](#ref-15) | Digital test for clinics | Sold to clinics | Yes: digital cognitive assessment for clinicians | No (for patients at home) | Doctors and clinics |

> Read the privacy policy and the terms of each product.

<br>

### 3.3 Strengths of the competitors

1. **BrainHQ** has the strongest trial evidence. Its speed exercise comes from the ACTIVE trial [[6]](#ref-6).
2. **SAGE** is free, simple and studied. Ohio State reports that it found mild cognitive impairment earlier than the MMSE test in an 8-year study [[10]](#ref-10).
3. **BrainCheck** is made for clinics. It is marketed as an FDA Class II medical device [[15]](#ref-15).
4. **Lumosity, Elevate and CogniFit** have many games, a large number of users and well-developed apps.

<br>

### 3.4 Weaknesses of the competitors

1. Most training products need a paid subscription for all functions [[11]](#ref-11) [[12]](#ref-12) [[13]](#ref-13) [[14]](#ref-14).
2. The training apps do not include a regular memory screening, except CogniFit (paid) [[12]](#ref-12).
3. The screening tools (SAGE, BrainCheck) do not include daily training.
4. Claims about dementia prevention need strong evidence. The FTC case against Lumosity shows this risk [[8]](#ref-8).

<br>

### 3.5 The gap that aivOavi fills

aivOavi is the only tool in this comparison that has all of these properties:

1. It is free.
2. It has screening **and** daily training **and** a trend in one tool.
3. It has a special Easy mode for persons with early dementia.
4. It needs no account. All data stays locked on the device.
5. It works in a web browser. There is no installation and no app store.

<br>

### 3.6 Risks for aivOavi

1. aivOavi has no clinical validation. The competitors BrainHQ, SAGE and BrainCheck have more evidence.
2. aivOavi must not claim that it prevents or diagnoses dementia. The FTC case [[8]](#ref-8) shows the legal risk of such claims.
3. A free product without a server has no income. A future plan for funding is necessary (for example a health organization or a research grant).

## 4. How to use aivOavi

### 4.1 Set up aivOavi

| ⏱ Time | 🔁 How often |
|---|---|
| Approximately 1 minute | One time only |

> **▶ START** &nbsp;—&nbsp; Open aivOavi in the web browser of your phone, tablet or computer.

1. Find the text **▼ START HERE**.
2. Touch **▶ Start**.
3. **Step 1 of 5:** select one path:
   - **Check my memory**
   - **Keep my brain strong**
   - **Daily brain training** (this path turns on Easy mode)
4. **Step 2 of 5:** type your first name or a nickname. Touch **Continue**.
5. **Step 3 of 5:** select a reminder time (**Morning**, **Afternoon** or **Evening**). If you do not want reminders, touch **No reminders**.
6. **Step 4 of 5:** if you want a PIN, touch **Add a PIN**. Type a PIN with 4 to 6 numbers two times. If you do not want a PIN, touch **Not now**.
7. **Step 5 of 5:** read the text about your privacy. This is the last step.
8. Touch **Go to my home screen**.

> **■ END** &nbsp;—&nbsp; You see the home screen. If you selected *Check my memory*, you see the Brain Check-up. Go to [4.3 Do the Brain Check-up](#43-do-the-brain-check-up).

> [!TIP]
> You can install aivOavi like an app:
> 1. On a phone, open the browser menu and select **Add to Home screen**.
> 2. On a computer, click the install icon in the address bar.
>
> Then aivOavi opens on the full screen and works without the internet.

<br>

### 4.2 Play today's games

| ⏱ Time | 🔁 How often |
|---|---|
| 5 to 10 minutes | One time each day |

> **▶ START** &nbsp;—&nbsp; Open aivOavi. You see the home screen.

1. Find the text **▼ START HERE**.
2. Touch **▶ Play today's games**.
3. Read the **How to play** card. At the top, you see **Game 1 of 3**.
4. Touch **▶ Start the game**.
5. Play the game.
6. Look at your stars and points.
7. Touch **➜ Next game**.
8. Do steps 3 to 7 again for game 2 and game 3.

> **■ END** &nbsp;—&nbsp; After game 3, you see **■ You are finished for today**. Touch **🏠 Go to Home**. You can close aivOavi now. Come back tomorrow.

> [!NOTE]
> If you want to play more, touch **All games** on the home screen. This is not necessary.

**The 8 games:**

| # | Game | Brain area | What you do |
|:-:|---|---|---|
| 1 | 🃏 Memory Match | Short-term memory | Look at the cards. Then find the pairs. |
| 2 | 🔆 Pattern Echo | Working memory | Watch the tiles. Then touch them in the same order. |
| 3 | 🌷 Word Garden | Word memory | Remember the words. Then find them in a list. |
| 4 | 🔢 Number Path | Attention | Touch 1, 2, 3… in order. On higher levels: 1, A, 2, B… |
| 5 | 🔍 Odd One Out | Reasoning | Touch the item that is different. |
| 6 | 🕰️ Clock Setter | Space and shapes | Move the clock hands to the given time. |
| 7 | 📅 Today Quiz | Orientation | Answer questions about the day, month, season and year. |
| 8 | 🙂 Face & Name | Names and faces | Meet new faces. Then find their names. |

<br>

### 4.3 Do the Brain Check-up

| ⏱ Time | 🔁 How often |
|---|---|
| Approximately 8 minutes | One time every 4 weeks |

> [!WARNING]
> The check-up is not a medical diagnosis. Only a doctor can diagnose dementia.

> **▶ START** &nbsp;—&nbsp; Sit in a quiet place. Open aivOavi. On the home screen, touch **🩺 Brain Check-up**.

1. Read the text. Find **▼ START HERE**. Touch **▶ Start**.
2. **Part 1 of 6:** remember the 5 words. Touch **I remember them**.
3. **Part 2 of 6:** answer the 4 questions about today.
4. **Part 3 of 6:** touch the circles in order. Do this two times.
5. **Part 4 of 6:** set the clock to the given time. Touch **Check**. Do this two times.
6. **Part 5 of 6:** touch **Start the timer**. Touch all the animals. Then touch **Done**.
7. **Part 6 of 6:** touch the 5 words from part 1. Then touch **Done**.
8. Read your result:
   - **Green ✅** means "Your results are good!".
   - **Orange ⚠️** means "Some areas need attention". Speak to your doctor.
9. If you want to give the result to your doctor, touch **🖨️ Print summary for my doctor**.

> **■ END** &nbsp;—&nbsp; You see **■ The check-up is finished**. You can close aivOavi now.

<br>

### 4.4 Look at your progress

> **▶ START** &nbsp;—&nbsp; On the home screen, touch **📈 My Brain Journey**.

1. Read the card at the top:
   - **Green** means your scores are stable or go up.
   - **Orange** means your scores are lower. Speak to your doctor.
2. Look at the chart. Touch **Week**, **Month** or **All** to change the time period.
3. To see a value, touch a dot on the chart.
4. Look at the score for each brain area and the list of check-ups.

> **■ END** &nbsp;—&nbsp; Touch **🏠 Home**.

<br>

### 4.5 Set a daily reminder

> **▶ START** &nbsp;—&nbsp; On the home screen, touch **⚙️ Settings**.

1. Find **Daily reminder**.
2. Select a time: **Morning**, **Afternoon** or **Evening**.
3. Touch **Turn on notifications**. If the browser asks for permission, touch **Allow**.
4. Touch **📅 Add daily reminder to my calendar**.
5. Open the file. Your calendar adds a daily event.

> **■ END** &nbsp;—&nbsp; Your calendar shows the reminder every day. Touch **🏠 Home**.

> [!TIP]
> The calendar reminder is the most reliable reminder. It works on all phones, also when aivOavi is closed. Read [7. Reminders: what works](#7-reminders-what-works).

<br>

### 4.6 Use the Caregiver area

> **▶ START** &nbsp;—&nbsp; On the home screen, touch **⚙️ Settings**. Then touch **Caregiver area**.

> [!NOTE]
> In Easy mode, the **⚙️ Settings** button opens the Caregiver area directly.

1. Answer the question (for example: "What is 5 + 3?"). If there is a PIN, type the PIN.
2. Select the task that you want to do:

| # | Task | What to touch |

## 6. Privacy and security

1. aivOavi has **no server, no accounts, no cookies, no analytics, no advertisements and no external fonts**. The app loads files only from its own address. The tests check this.
2. **The app locks (encrypts) all data on the device.** It uses AES-GCM-256 (Web Crypto):
   - Without a PIN, the key is a **non-extractable** `CryptoKey`. No script can read the key.
   - With a PIN, PBKDF2-SHA256 (600,000 iterations) and a random salt protect the key. Without the PIN, nobody can read the data.
   - After 5 wrong PINs, you must wait. The wait time increases after each wrong PIN.
   - When you add or remove a PIN, the app locks all data again with a new key.
3. **The Content Security Policy is strict:** `default-src 'self'`. There are no inline scripts and no inline styles. The app sets all text with `textContent`. It never uses `innerHTML`.
4. **A password that you select locks each backup** (PBKDF2 + AES-GCM).
5. **Delete all my data** deletes IndexedDB, the caches, the service worker and the local storage.
6. Only 2 values are not locked: the reminder time and the date of your last game. The service worker needs these values. They contain no health data and no scores.
7. **Notifications never show scores or health data.**

## 7. Reminders: what works

A browser cannot open a closed web app without a push server. aivOavi has no server, because this keeps your data private. Thus aivOavi uses 4 types of reminder:

| # | Reminder | When it works |
|:-:|---|---|
| 1 | **In-app reminder** | A notification at the reminder time, when aivOavi is open or in a background tab. |
| 2 | **Periodic Background Sync** | The browser opens the app approximately one time each day. This works only for an *installed* app in Chrome, Edge or Android. The browser decides the exact time. |
| 3 | **"Welcome back" message** | The app shows this message when you open it after a pause. |
| 4 | **Calendar reminder** | A daily event in your own calendar (`.ics` file). This works on all devices, also on iPhone, also when aivOavi is closed. **This is the most reliable reminder.** |

> [!NOTE]
> If you want push notifications in the future, add a small push server (for example Node + `web-push`). The server must keep only an anonymous subscription. You do not need to change the health data part of the app.

### 8.4 Put aivOavi on the internet

> **▶ START** &nbsp;—&nbsp; Make sure that your web host uses **HTTPS**. The app needs HTTPS.

1. Copy the `aivoavi` folder to a static web host (for example Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, GitHub Pages or nginx). You do not need the folders `tests/`, `tools/` and `docs/` on the web host.
2. Set the security headers:
   - **Netlify and Cloudflare Pages:** these hosts read the `_headers` file automatically. Do nothing.
   - **GitHub Pages:** this host cannot set headers. The `<meta>` tag in `index.html` gives the strict CSP. Do nothing.
   - **nginx:** copy each header from `_headers` into an `add_header` line.
3. Open the web address of the app in a browser.

> **■ END** &nbsp;—&nbsp; You see the aivOavi welcome screen.

> [!CAUTION]
> When you change an app file, increase `VERSION` in `sw.js` (for example from `aivoavi-v2` to `aivoavi-v3`). If you do not do this, installed apps keep the old files.

### 8.5 Features (technical)

1. **8 games** (`js/games/`). All games use the same interface: `mount(el, ctx)` → `onDone({score})`.
2. **Adaptive difficulty** (`js/core/adaptive.js`): levels 1 to 6. In Easy mode, the maximum is level 3. The goal is approximately 80% correct answers.
3. **Daily plan** (`js/core/plan.js`): 3 games each day. The plan changes the brain areas, adds the weakest area and does not repeat the games of the last 2 days.
4. **Game elements** (`js/core/gamify.js`): XP, 5 levels (Seed → Mighty Oak), days in a row (1 rest day each week is accepted), Brain Garden (1 plant for each complete day) and 10 badges.
5. **Trends** (`js/core/scoring.js`, `screens/progress.js`): a line chart of the brain score, small charts for each brain area and the check-up history:
   - The card is **orange** when the 4-week average is 10% (or more) lower than the starting point for 3 weeks.
   - In all other conditions, the card is **neon green**.
   - The score includes the difficulty level. Thus an easier game cannot hide a real decrease.
6. **Brain Check-up** (`screens/check.js`): word memory (later recall), orientation, two trail tasks (attention), clock (space and shapes) and animal search (word finding).
7. **Start and end markers** (`marker()` in `js/ui/components.js`): **▼ START HERE** above the first button, **Step / Game / Part n of N** on each step, and **■ finished** at the end.
8. **Text** (`js/i18n/en.js`): all text of the app is in this file.
9. **Accessibility:** text 22 to 30 px, buttons minimum 72 px, icon and word on each button, no swipes, no long presses, 🔊 read aloud (device voice only), calm mode, `prefers-reduced-motion`, screen reader labels and a large number keypad.

<br>

### 8.6 Add a language

1. Copy `js/i18n/en.js` to a new file, for example `de.js`.
2. Translate the text in the new file.
3. Import the new file in `js/i18n/t.js`.
